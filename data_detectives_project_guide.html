🔎 Data Detectives

Data All Around Us · Project guide · Presentations Nov 10–11

Your Mission

You are a Data Detective. Pick a question about the world around you. Then collect real data to answer it, find out what the numbers say, and share your discovery with the class. You will work on your own and choose how to present. The most important part is collecting good data over time, not just one day.

What You Will Do

  1. Pick your question. Choose something you can count or measure.
  2. Make a collection plan. Decide what data you need, where it comes from, and how many (aim for 10 or more).
  3. Collect your data. Gather it over a few days. Write each number down right away.
  4. Put your data in order. Line the numbers up from smallest to biggest.
  5. Find the center. Figure out the mean, median, and mode. Show your work.
  6. Round & estimate. Round your mean and write one “about” sentence.
  7. Make a chart. Build a bar chart of your data.
  8. Write your data story. Say what you found in 2–3 sentences.
  9. Choose how to share and present.

You will complete Steps 1–8 in your Data Workbook on paper or on the computer.

How to Collect Good Data

  • Pick a question you can answer with numbers.
  • Decide who or what you will measure and how many you need.
  • Ask the same question the same way every time so it is fair.
  • Write each number down as soon as you get it.
  • Keep going until you have at least 10 pieces of data.

Examples: how many pets each person has; minutes read each night; height in centimeters; number of siblings; favorite number of something you can count.

Detective Tips

  • Pick a question you really want to know the answer to.
  • Always put your numbers in order before finding the median.
  • Check your chart: do the tallest bars match the biggest numbers?
  • Practice your presentation out loud at least once.
  • When stuck, ask for help — good detectives ask good questions.
